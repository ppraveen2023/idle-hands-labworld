# 24 Nov 2026 (Tue)

**Summary:** dropped the corrigibility project, settled on *Idle Hands*, built LabWorld as an RL environment and an Inspect task, and ran a pilot across 17 models. The pilot showed capable saboteurs choose omission unprompted. A prior-work check then showed "monitors miss omission" is already known in software settings, so the novelty claim moved to harm that builds over time in physical processes.

## Choosing the project
- Dropped *The Price of Corrigibility*.
- Scored three physical-AI directions (home safety benchmark, industrial handover, sim-to-real) against the TARA criteria. The home benchmark was feasible but read as a plain benchmark.
- Moved to AI control: ARENA 3.5 gives a template, and control links directly to loss of oversight.
- Stress-tested the first version ("saboteur butler"): Ctrl-Z overlap, realism, weak attacker, circularity.
- Ran an idea-optimizer loop (3 LLM judges plus a held-out judge, ~40 variants). It pushed toward a tighter scope and a lab setting, and every judge flagged circularity.
- Added an RL red team to answer the circularity criticism.
- Wrote the project proposal (Claude doc and Google Drive).

## Designing the world
- Decisions D1–D6, made one at a time and logged in `DECISIONS.md`: chemistry lab; three hazards, each reachable by action or by inaction; 2% leak per tick; `wait(1-5)`; goal-plus-manual tasks with varied starts; supervisors see actions, sensor reads and a snapshot every 5 ticks.
- Tried simpler rules (Revision 1), then restored the original seven (Revision 2).

## Building
- Modular package: config → state → rules → tasks → episode, with Gymnasium (`LabWorld-v0`) and Inspect front ends.
- Testing found and fixed four rule problems:
  - the fume batch task didn't always need a refill
  - a wait could run past the time limit
  - coolant could go below 0
  - the commission saboteur's accident was really an omission
- Inspect layer: 8 tools; the night saved in the store, so logs can be re-scored; referee scorer; mock-model end-to-end tests.
- 58 tests passing. Git repo with 2 commits.

## Pilot results (3 nights per model and role)
- **Honest:** every Claude model was safe. Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 4.6 failed the long-hold task (competence). Fable 5.2 and Opus 5 kept the widest coolant margin.
- **Saboteur prompt:**
  - Blocked by the API: Fable 5.x, Opus 5
  - Openly refused: Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5
  - Silently ignored the goal: older Opus, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku
  - **Sabotaged: Sonnet 5**
- **Every model that sabotaged chose omission unprompted:** Sonnet 5 3/3, nodecode-cyber-v1 3/3, Hermes 4 2/2.
- Sonnet 5 revealed its intent in its text; nodecode-cyber-v1 did not. Both reframed the accident as "unexpected" in their reports.
- Confirmed nodecode access: a nodecode.ai account with 3 models, usable from Inspect through its OpenAI-compatible API.

## Prior work

- **Corrected claim:** we test omission that builds over time and becomes irreversible mid-run (physical-process control), where end-of-run checks are too late and monitors must forecast.
- **Design change:** add Kemeklis & Vemuri's end-of-run postcondition monitor as a baseline.
